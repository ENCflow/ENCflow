# test/tjunction — T-junction of one-cell-wide channels (check of the junction loss)

English mirror of test/tjunction/README.md

[日本語](../README.md)

At a junction where a tributary of the same width enters a one-cell-wide main channel at a right
angle, the depth in the main channel just upstream of the junction (the backwater caused by the
junction) is compared with a one-dimensional theory. The test shows how much of the loss needed to
accelerate the tributary water in the direction of the main channel is counted by the
momentum-conserving advection term (`f_advection_scheme = 3` in `&list_enc`;
[Advection schemes](../../../docs/en/users_guide/advection.md)).

There are two junction shapes (a junction along the grid axes and a junction rotated 45° to the
grid) and three sets of advection rules (the treatments of the water entering through the side faces
`f_advection_lateral = 0` and `1`, and the combination of four values for deep, slow neighbouring
cells), so six groups are computed.

- **`f_advection_lateral = 0` (the default until 2026-11-16) counts this loss only while the banks are wet.** Only
  what both rules satisfy, such as steadiness and the mass balance, is judged; the difference from
  the theory and the difference caused by the wetness of the banks are displayed as "the limit
  of 0".
- **`f_advection_lateral = 1` (the default) counts it regardless of the wetness of the banks.** The difference in
  backwater between wet and dry banks, the position within the theory, and the independence from the
  grid orientation are judged as well.
- **The combination of four values (`f_advection_donor = 4`, `f_advection_bend = 2`,
  `f_advection_lateral = 2`, `f_dry_head_cap = 4`; optional) also counts it regardless of the wetness of
  the banks.** The same items as with 1 are judged (only the range of the position within the theory
  for the junction along the axes differs).

## Setup

Slope 0.002, n = 0.03, Δx = 50 m. Outside the channels the banks are ground 30 m above the bed,
which the water does not climb. The discharges are given as sources in the upstream end cells of the
main channel and the tributary, raised over 10 minutes and then held constant. dt = 1 s, 2 hours.
The discharge downstream of the junction is 1,000 m³/s in every configuration. The input is
generated by `make_tjunction.py`.

| Shape | Grid | Main channel and tributary | Cell spacing, passage width | Downstream of the junction: unit-width discharge, normal depth, Froude number |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Junction along the axes | 80 × 25 | The main channel flows east along row 13; the tributary flows toward it along rows 1–12 of column 40. The junction cell is (40, 13). The east side is a free outflow | 50 m, 50 m | 20 m²/s, 4.75 m, 0.62 |
| Junction rotated 45° | 100 × 100 | The main channel follows the diagonal (k, k) toward increasing k; the tributary follows the diagonal (35 + m, 35 − m), m = 1–25, toward it. The junction cell is (35, 35). The east side is a free outflow | 70.7 m, 35.4 m | 28.3 m²/s, 5.85 m, 0.64 |

The passage width of 35.4 m of the rotated junction is the width of a one-cell diagonal channel (the
natural diagonal width Δx Δy / √(Δx² + Δy²)).

Configuration names are `<group prefix><discharge ratio>_<banks>`.

| Group prefix | Shape | Advection rules |
|---|---|---|
| (none) | Junction along the axes | `f_advection_lateral = 0` |
| `l1_` | Junction along the axes | `f_advection_lateral = 1` |
| `c4_` | Junction along the axes | Combination of four values |
| `r_` | Junction rotated 45° | `f_advection_lateral = 0` |
| `rl1_` | Junction rotated 45° | `f_advection_lateral = 1` |
| `rc4_` | Junction rotated 45° | Combination of four values |

In the groups with `f_advection_lateral = 0` and `1`, the other three switches state the combination of the old values
(`f_advection_donor = 3`, `f_advection_bend = 1`, `f_dry_head_cap = 3`). Every group states all four switches (none
depends on the defaults).

| Discharge ratio | Main / tributary discharge (m³/s) |
|---|---|
| `m800` | 800 / 200 (main channel dominant) |
| `m500` | 500 / 500 |
| `m200` | 200 / 800 (tributary dominant) |

The banks are `dry` or `wet`. In the wet-bank configurations, sources of 0.05 m³/s each are placed
in five bank cells around the junction cell (the two on either side of the tributary mouth and the
three on the opposite bank of the main channel), wetting the banks with a thin film (0.03 % of the
discharge).

In addition, `l1_m500_wet`, `rl1_m500_wet`, `c4_m500_wet` and `rc4_m500_wet` are computed for
30 minutes in twelve configurations mirrored east–west (`_mx`), north–south (`_my`) and both (`_xy`);
in the configurations mirrored east–west the west side is the free outflow. There are
48 configurations in total.

## Theory

The depth in the main channel one cell upstream of the junction (J−1) is solved from the computed
depth two cells downstream of the junction (J+2). The reach is three cells, and the bed-slope and
friction terms are integrated by the trapezoidal rule using the computed depths (rectangular
section, per unit width; $`q_1`$ is the unit-width discharge of the main channel upstream and
$`q_3`$ that downstream of the junction).

- **Momentum theory**: the tributary water entering at a right angle is taken to carry no momentum
  in the direction of the main channel.

  ```math
  \frac{h_1^2}{2} + \frac{q_1^2}{g h_1} + \int (S_0 - S_f)\,h\,dx = \frac{h_3^2}{2} + \frac{q_3^2}{g h_3}
  ```

- **No-loss theory**: no energy is lost at the junction (lower bound).

  ```math
  h_1 + \frac{q_1^2}{2 g h_1^2} = h_3 + \frac{q_3^2}{2 g h_3^2} + h_f - S_0 L
  ```

The momentum equation is the standard treatment of open-channel junctions (Taylor 1944; experiments
on right-angle equal-width junctions in Hsu et al. 1998). Adding the bed-slope and friction terms
over the junction reach and integrating them with the computed depths is a simplification of this
test. The position of the computed depth $`h`$ between the two theories is expressed as
$`p = (h - h_E)/(h_M - h_E)`$ (0 = no loss, 1 = momentum theory).

## Check items (Check_tjunction.py)

Items judged in all six groups:

1. Steadiness: the range of the depth over the last 10 minutes is below 0.03 m (the six cells on
   either side of the junction in the main channel and the four cells of the tributary before the
   junction).
2. Mass balance: the discharge at the transect 30 cells downstream of the junction is within ±0.2 %
   of the sum of the given discharges.
3. The depth six cells downstream of the junction is within ±1 % of the normal depth.
4. No energy gain at the junction: the depth at J−1 is not more than 0.03 m below the no-loss
   theory.

In the groups with `f_advection_lateral = 0`, the tolerances of items 1 and 2 are 0.05 m and ±1 %:
with wet banks the backwater of the rotated junction is 1 m higher, and the water stored upstream has
not finished accumulating after 2 hours.

Items judged in the groups with `f_advection_lateral = 1` and in the groups with the combination of
four values, and displayed in the groups with 0 (the limit of 0):

5. The difference in depth at J−1 between wet and dry banks is below 0.02 m (the backwater of the
   junction does not depend on the presence of a film on the banks).
6. The position $`p`$ is 0.5–0.7 for the junction along the axes (0.4–0.7 with the combination of
   four values) and 0.7–0.95 for the rotated junction.

Item judged in the mirrored configurations:

7. The depths at the 17 probes at 30 minutes are the same as those of the base orientation at the
   same time (difference below 5 × 10⁻⁶ m; the rule does not depend on the grid orientation).

(The ranges of the theory come from taking the depths for the friction term from the computed
profile of each configuration.)

- **With `f_advection_lateral = 0` and dry banks, the junction loss is hardly counted.** The depth of
  the junction along the axes is 0.34–0.41 m below the momentum theory, and for the
  main-channel-dominant junction it is almost the same as the no-loss theory. For the rotated
  junction it equals the no-loss theory for all three discharge ratios. ENCflow treats a face whose
  upwind donor is a wall or dry bed as neutral, and uses the path value for flow that turns in
  (`f_advection_bend`). Both are treatments for carrying the staircase of a channel oblique to the
  grid as a straight channel, and they act the same way at a real junction.
- **With 0 and wet banks, the slow velocity of the film links on the banks is used as the velocity
  of the water entering from the side.** This is not counted as a physical rule, so the values
  scatter with the discharge ratio and the grid orientation (p = 0.32–1.63), and the presence of a
  film on the banks changes the backwater of the junction by 0.14–1.09 m. The water level just
  upstream of a junction therefore differs between a computation with rain and one without (see §7
  of [One-cell-wide channels and the upwind donors of the advection term](../../../docs/en/users_guide/advection_donor.md)
  in the users guide).
- **With 1, the backwater is 60–80 % of that of the momentum theory regardless of the wetness of the
  banks.** The difference between wet and dry banks is below 0.01 m, and p is 0.57–0.62 for the
  junction along the axes and 0.81–0.84 for the rotated junction. The mirrored configurations give
  the same values as the base orientation (item 7).
- **With the combination of four values, the backwater of the junction along the axes is 0.01–0.05 m
  smaller than with 1** (p = 0.47–0.57; 0.57 → 0.47 for the main-channel-dominant junction). The
  rotated junction gives the same values as with 1. In both shapes no energy is gained at the junction
  (item 4), the difference between wet and dry banks is below 0.01 m, and the mirrored configurations
  give the same values as the base orientation. What moves the values of the junction along the axes is
  `f_advection_bend = 2` (a computation with only this value set to 2 gives the same depths as the
  combination of four values). A computation with only `f_advection_donor = 4` added falls to p = 0.22
  for the tributary-dominant junction, and becomes 0.57 when `f_advection_bend = 2` is added (the four
  values are meant to be used together).

## Limits and how to read the results

- **The backwater with 1 and with the combination of four values is smaller than that of the momentum
  theory and changes with the grid orientation** (p = 0.5–0.6 and 0.8). An eight-neighbour grid represents a right-angle junction as a
  combination of perpendicular links and diagonal links. At the junction along the axes, water also
  crosses from the last cell of the tributary to the downstream cell of the main channel through a
  diagonal link, and that water is counted as carrying cos 45° times the momentum of the main-channel
  velocity (a deficit of (1 − cos 45°) times). At the rotated junction the tributary water enters
  only through the perpendicular link. Counting the water of the diagonal links as carrying no
  momentum would bring the result closer to the theory, but the pond at a junction then keeps
  oscillating, so it is not adopted (developer.md §110.23 and §110.25). The depths are 0.08–0.25 m
  (1–4 %; 0.08–0.28 m with the combination of four values) below the momentum theory.
- The theory is one-dimensional. The separation zone and contraction downstream of the junction, the
  water-surface profile of the tributary and the effect of the junction angle are not included.
- Only one-cell-wide channels are examined. A junction where a narrow tributary enters a main channel
  several cells wide needs a separate test.
- Items 5 and 6 are not judged in the groups with 0, so the groups with 0 passing does not mean that
  the water level at a junction is correct.

## Running

```bash
make
./Run.sh          # serial (48 configurations; about 3.5 minutes on 4 threads)
./Run_MPI.sh 2    # MPI (with np = 2 the band boundary of the junction along the axes lies between the main channel and the tributary mouth)
```
