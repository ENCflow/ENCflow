# test/tjunction — T-junction of one-cell-wide channels (check of the junction loss and record of the current limit)

English mirror of test/tjunction/README.md

[日本語](../README.md)

At a junction where a tributary of the same width enters a one-cell-wide main channel at a right
angle, the depth in the main channel just upstream of the junction (the backwater caused by the
junction) is compared with a one-dimensional theory. The test shows how much of the loss needed to
accelerate the tributary water in the direction of the main channel is counted by the
momentum-conserving advection term (`f_advection_scheme = 3` in `&list_enc`;
[Advection schemes](../../../docs/en/users_guide/advection.md)).

**At present ENCflow counts this loss only while the banks are wet.** The test judges only what the
current code satisfies, such as steadiness and the mass balance, and displays the difference from the
theory as a "current limit" (not judged). A rule that counts the junction loss regardless of the
wetness of the banks is a development item; once it is in, the displayed items will become judged
items (developer.md §110.19 and §110.22).

## Setup

Grid 80 × 25, Δx = 50 m, slope 0.002, n = 0.03. The main channel flows east along row 13, and the
tributary flows toward the main channel along rows 1–12 of column 40 (same slope). The junction cell
is (40, 13). Outside the channels the banks are flat ground 30 m above the bed, which the water does
not climb. The discharges are given as sources in the upstream end cells of the main channel and the
tributary, raised over 10 minutes and then held constant. The east side is a free outflow, dt = 1 s,
2 hours. The discharge downstream of the junction is 1,000 m³/s in every configuration (20 m²/s per
unit width, normal depth 4.75 m, Froude number 0.62). The input is generated by `make_tjunction.py`.

| Configuration | File | Main / tributary discharge (m³/s) | Banks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | `param_m800_dry.txt`, `param_m800_wet.txt` | 800 / 200 (main channel dominant) | dry, wet |
| 3, 4 | `param_m500_dry.txt`, `param_m500_wet.txt` | 500 / 500 | dry, wet |
| 5, 6 | `param_m200_dry.txt`, `param_m200_wet.txt` | 200 / 800 (tributary dominant) | dry, wet |

In the wet-bank configurations, sources of 0.05 m³/s each are placed in five bank cells around the
junction cell (the two on either side of the tributary mouth and the three on the opposite bank of the
main channel), wetting the banks with a thin film (0.03 % of the discharge).

## Theory

The depth in the main channel one cell upstream of the junction (J−1) is solved from the computed
depth two cells downstream of the junction (J+2). The reach is three cells = 150 m, and the bed-slope
and friction terms are integrated by the trapezoidal rule using the computed depths (rectangular
section, per unit width; $`q_1`$ is the unit-width discharge of the main channel upstream and
$`q_3`$ that downstream of the junction).

- **Momentum theory**: the tributary water entering at a right angle is taken to carry no momentum
  in the direction of the main channel.

  ```math
  \frac{h_1^2}{2} + \frac{q_1^2}{g h_1} + \int (S_0 - S_f)\,h\,dx = \frac{h_3^2}{2} + \frac{q_3^2}{g h_3}
  ```

- **No-loss theory**: no energy is lost at the junction (lower bound).

  ```math
  h_1 + \frac{q_1^2}{2 g h_1^2} = h_3 + \frac{q_3^2}{2 g h_3^2} + h_f - S_0 L
  ```

The momentum equation is the standard treatment of open-channel junctions (Taylor 1944; experiments
on right-angle equal-width junctions in Hsu et al. 1998). Adding the bed-slope and friction terms
over the junction reach and integrating them with the computed depths is a simplification of this
test. The position of the computed depth $`h`$ between the two theories is expressed as
$`p = (h - h_E)/(h_M - h_E)`$ (0 = no loss, 1 = momentum theory).

## Check items (Check_tjunction.py)

Judged items (all six configurations):

1. Steadiness: the range of the depth over the last 10 minutes is below 0.03 m (the six cells on
   either side of the junction in the main channel and the four cells of the tributary before the
   junction).
2. Mass balance: the discharge at the transect 30 cells downstream of the junction is within ±0.2 %
   of the sum of the given discharges.
3. The depth six cells downstream of the junction is within ±1 % of the normal depth.
4. No energy gain at the junction: the depth at J−1 is not more than 0.03 m below the no-loss
   theory.

Recorded item (not judged):

5. The position $`p`$, the difference in depth from the momentum theory, and the difference in depth
   between wet and dry banks.

## Current values (item 5; the current limit)

| Main / tributary (m³/s) | Theory: no loss / momentum (m) | Dry banks: depth (m), $`p`$ | Wet banks: depth (m), $`p`$ | Wet − dry (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 800 / 200 | 5.03–5.06 / 5.36–5.41 | 5.06, 0.01 | 5.57, 1.63 | +0.51 |
| 500 / 500 | 5.33 / 5.87–5.89 | 5.48, 0.27 | 5.93, 1.11 | +0.45 |
| 200 / 800 | 5.46 / 6.10 | 5.75, 0.45 | 6.10, 0.99 | +0.35 |

(The ranges of the theory come from taking the depths for the friction term from the computed
profile of each configuration.)

- **With dry banks the junction loss is hardly counted.** The depth is 0.34–0.41 m (6–7 %) below the
  momentum theory, and for the main-channel-dominant junction it is almost the same as the no-loss
  theory. ENCflow treats a face whose upwind donor is a wall or dry bed as neutral, and uses the path
  value for flow that turns in (`f_advection_bend`). Both are treatments for carrying the staircase
  of a channel oblique to the grid as a straight channel, and they act the same way at a real
  junction.
- **With wet banks the depth is close to the momentum theory** (+0.00 to +0.21 m). This is because
  the slow velocity of the film links on the banks is used as the velocity of the water entering from
  the side; it is not counted as a physical rule. It matches the theory for the tributary-dominant
  junction and overshoots for the main-channel-dominant junction.
- = **The presence of a film on the banks changes the backwater of the junction by 0.35–0.51 m.** The
  water level just upstream of a junction therefore differs between a computation with rain and one
  without (see "Lateral-face braking remains" in §7 of
  [One-cell-wide channels and the upwind donors of the advection term](../../../docs/en/users_guide/advection_donor.md)
  in the users guide).

## Limits and how to read the results

- The theory is one-dimensional. The separation zone and contraction downstream of the junction, the
  water-surface profile of the tributary and the effect of the junction angle are not included. On
  the ENC grid, water also crosses from the last cell of the tributary to the downstream cell of the
  main channel through a diagonal link, so a right-angle junction is partly represented as an oblique
  one.
- Only one-cell-wide channels are examined. A junction where a narrow tributary enters a main channel
  several cells wide needs a separate test.
- Item 5 is not judged, so passing this test does not mean that the water level at a junction is
  correct.

## Running

```bash
make
./Run.sh          # serial (6 configurations; about 15 s on 4 threads)
./Run_MPI.sh 2    # MPI (with np = 2 the band boundary lies between the main channel and the tributary mouth)
```
