!
! Example of internal hydraulic structure settings
!   &list_struct_pump      : drainage pumps
!   &list_struct_culvert   : culverts (sluice pipes and sluice gates are
!                            represented with the gate extension)
!   &list_struct_diversion : diversions (out-of-catchment diversion /
!                            intake weirs)
!   &list_struct_dam       : dams and lakes (capture set absorption +
!                            bucket storage + release by an operating rule)
!
!   Use this file by specifying, in the system parameters &list_sysparam,
!     fn_structure = "list_structure.txt"
!   (if fn_structure is not specified, internal hydraulic structures are
!   completely inactive).
!
!   The internal hydraulic structure family shares a common skeleton:
!     two cell sets (intake side -> outlet side or out of the domain)
!     + a hydraulic law Q (a pure function of the reference value)
!     + mass-conservative transfer
!   Write the groups of each type in this same file. If a group itself
!   is not written, that type is disabled (absence is normal).
!
!   Cell coordinates (i, j) are 1-based (the westernmost column and the
!   northernmost row are 1). GIS software such as QGIS counts rasters
!   from 0, so add +1 to both the row and column numbers picked in GIS.
!
!   Structure numbers n are consecutive from 1 per type (a gap stops
!   with an error). Up to 50 structures per type, 999 cells per
!   structure, and 999 polyline points.
!
!   Note: drainage pumps were moved from &list_bound_pump of fn_boundary
!   to &list_struct_pump of this file (2026-08-07; parameter names
!   unchanged). An &list_bound_pump written at the old location stops the
!   program with migration guidance when detected.
!
!======================================================================
! Drainage pumps (group absent = no pumps)
!   Forced transfer (mechanical drainage) from an intake cell set to an
!   outlet cell set. Intake and outlet need not be adjacent; the distance
!   is unlimited. Omitting the outlet drains out of the domain (pumping
!   to sewers or another catchment = removal from the system). The
!   transfer is mass conservative, and a limit to the water actually
!   present on the intake side applies every step (the travel delay of
!   the pumping is not represented).
!   The operating rule is exactly one of:
!     pump_q0(n)     : constant discharge at all times (m3/s)
!     pump_rule(:,k,n): a reference level-discharge polyline (reference
!                       value m, discharge m3/s). Linear interpolation;
!                       end values outside the range. A simple threshold
!                       ON/OFF is expressed by a steep ramp (two close
!                       points), and staged operation (adding pump units)
!                       by a staircase polyline. Being a pure function of
!                       the current level, there is no history state
!                       (restart unchanged). Start/stop hysteresis is not
!                       represented
!   The reference is f_pump_ref(n) = 0: water level eta=z+h (m) of the
!                         representative (first) intake cell
!                         1: depth h (m) of the same cell
!   Cell sets can also be given as files (from dir_data, each line "i j"):
!     fn_pump_in_cell(n) / fn_pump_out_cell(n)
!   Pond intake: when an intake cell has rscap > 0 (a pond), water is
!   drawn from the pond storage (hrs) instead of the field water surface
!   (forebay drainage). In that case the depth reference (f_pump_ref=1)
!   refers to the pond depth hrs. The water-level reference (=0) cannot
!   be used at a pond representative cell (stops with an error)
!   Note: f_bank_mode=2 (forced drainage) of the levee model (fn_channel)
!   is for compatibility with past runs and extreme-scenario experiments;
!   for real pumping stations use this family
!======================================================================
!&list_struct_pump
!  ! Pump 1: interior water to the main river; starts at forebay depth
!  ! 0.5 m and reaches the rating at 1.0 m
!  pump_in_cell(:,1,1)  = 120, 45        ! intake cell (multiple lines for a set)
!  pump_out_cell(:,1,1) = 95, 12         ! outlet cell
!  f_pump_ref(1) = 1                     ! reference = depth h of the
!                                        !   representative intake cell
!  pump_rule(:,1,1) = 0.5, 0.0           ! (depth m, discharge m3/s)
!  pump_rule(:,2,1) = 1.0, 5.0           !   ramp over 0.5-1.0 m, 5 m3/s beyond
!  ! Pump 2: constant 2 m3/s at all times, out of the domain (sewer)
!  pump_in_cell(:,1,2) = 200, 80
!  pump_q0(2) = 2.0
!/

!======================================================================
! Culverts (group absent = no culverts)
!   A pipe of rectangular section B x D connecting an upstream cell set
!   and a downstream cell set (both mandatory). Represents underdrains,
!   sluice pipes and sluice gates crossing embankments, roads and levees.
!   **Bidirectional**: water flows from the higher to the lower level,
!   and the flow reverses automatically if the levels reverse.
!   Gates (sluice pipes / sluice gates):
!     culv_flap(n) = 1     : flap gate (an unpowered check valve that
!                            blocks reverse flow out -> in)
!     culv_gate_rule(:,k,n): gate opening polyline (reference water level
!                            eta m, opening 0-1). Linear interpolation;
!                            end values outside the range. Unspecified =
!                            always fully open. The opening is a linear
!                            multiplier on the discharge (the nonlinearity
!                            of the area is approximated)
!     culv_gate_ref(n)     : reference cell of the opening rule
!                            (0: in-side representative, 1: out-side
!                            representative = river side. Default 1)
!   Discharge law (3 regimes; Sec. 22):
!     no flow      : upstream depth zero or below, or upstream level at
!                    or below the downstream invert elevation
!     free surface : Honma formula (width B; the same family as levee
!                    overtopping. Free/submerged switch at downstream
!                    depth ratio 2/3)
!     full pipe    : orifice formula
!                    Q = BD*sqrt(2g*deta/(1+ce+2g*n^2*L/R^(4/3)))
!                    (continuously connected by Q = min(weir, pipe))
!   The transfer per step is capped by "the volume that fills half the
!   representative level difference" (prevents back-and-forth oscillation
!   at coarse dt; Sec. 22).
!   Parameters per culvert (for each number n):
!     culv_in_cell / culv_out_cell : upstream and downstream cells (sets
!                                    allowed; file specification is
!                                    fn_culv_*_cell)
!     culv_width(n) / culv_height(n) : section B x D (m). Mandatory
!     culv_zin(n) / culv_zout(n)     : upstream and downstream invert
!                                      elevations (m). Mandatory
!     culv_length(n)  : pipe length L (m). Default 0 (no friction loss)
!     culv_manning(n) : in-pipe roughness n. Default 0.02
!     culv_ce(n)      : entrance loss coefficient. Default 0.5
!   Constraints: cannot connect to pond (rscap>0) cells. The downstream
!   side cannot be omitted (out of the domain) either (for out-of-domain
!   drainage use the pump pump_q0). The transfer is clear water (carries
!   no sediment). Flux transects (record) do not measure culvert
!   discharge.
!   Note (scale requirement): a configuration whose conveyance reaches
!   the scale of cell area x depth (a section too large for the cell
!   size) makes the point-source forcing on the receiving side exceed
!   the stability limit of the shallow water system and diverge
!   (Sec. 22). Use a scale realistic for the cell size and dt
!======================================================================
!&list_struct_culvert
!  ! Culvert 1: a 2m x 2m box culvert crossing a road embankment
!  culv_in_cell(:,1,1)  = 150, 60        ! upstream cell
!  culv_out_cell(:,1,1) = 152, 60        ! downstream cell
!  culv_width(1)  = 2.0                  ! section width B (m)
!  culv_height(1) = 2.0                  ! section height D (m)
!  culv_zin(1)  = 12.50                  ! upstream invert elevation (m)
!  culv_zout(1) = 12.30                  ! downstream invert elevation (m)
!  culv_length(1) = 25.0                 ! pipe length (m)
!  ! Culvert 2: a sluice gate (landside = in side -> river = out side).
!  ! A flap prevents reverse flow and, in addition, an operated gate
!  ! closes at river levels 8.0-8.5 m
!  culv_in_cell(:,1,2)  = 120, 45        ! landside cell
!  culv_out_cell(:,1,2) = 118, 45        ! river-side cell
!  culv_width(2)  = 1.5
!  culv_height(2) = 1.5
!  culv_zin(2)  = 6.20
!  culv_zout(2) = 6.00
!  culv_length(2) = 30.0
!  culv_flap(2) = 1                      ! check valve against reverse flow
!  culv_gate_ref(2) = 1                  ! reference = water level eta of the
!                                        !   river-side representative cell
!  culv_gate_rule(:,1,2) = 8.0, 1.0      ! (water level m, opening)
!  culv_gate_rule(:,2,2) = 8.5, 0.0      !   closing ramp over 8.0-8.5 m
!/

!======================================================================
! Diversions (group absent = no diversions)
!   One-way, passive withdrawal (intake weir / diversion channel) from an
!   intake cell set to out of the domain (out-of-catchment diversion
!   = the main use) or to a destination cell set. The rule is exactly
!   one of:
!     div_q0(n)      : constant discharge at all times (m3/s)
!     div_rule(:,k,n): a rating polyline of the water level eta of the
!                      representative (first) intake cell vs discharge
!                      (water level eta m, discharge m3/s). Linear
!                      interpolation; end values outside the range
!   Difference from pumps (passivity): when a destination is specified,
!   the diversion stops automatically while the water level of the
!   destination representative cell is at or above the intake side
!   (gravity cannot climb). With the out-of-domain specification
!   (div_out_cell omitted) this guard does not apply.
!   The reference is the water level eta only. Pond (rscap>0) cells are
!   not allowed (substitute a pump).
!   Cell sets can also be given as files: fn_div_in_cell(n) / fn_div_out_cell(n)
!   The limit to the water actually present on the intake side is shared
!   with the other structures
!======================================================================
!&list_struct_diversion
!  ! Diversion 1: diversion out of the catchment by an intake weir rating
!  ! (withdrawal starts at water level 10.2 m)
!  div_in_cell(:,1,1) = 80, 30           ! intake cell (just upstream of the weir)
!  div_rule(:,1,1) = 10.2, 0.0           ! (water level eta m, discharge m3/s)
!  div_rule(:,2,1) = 10.8, 20.0          !   ramp over 10.2-10.8 m, 20 m3/s beyond
!  ! Diversion 2: constant diversion to an irrigation canal (with a
!  ! destination = stops automatically when downstream is higher)
!  div_in_cell(:,1,2)  = 80, 30
!  div_out_cell(:,1,2) = 95, 60
!  div_q0(2) = 3.0
!/

!======================================================================
! Dams and lakes (group absent = none)
!   The cells of the water surface (capture set) absorb and store all
!   arriving water every step (the storage state is shared with s%hrs =
!   save/restore is complete with the existing mechanism), and release
!   to the release cell set according to the operating rule. The
!   impounded surface (flow and backwater on the lake) is not solved
!   (a bucket model). Not only dam reservoirs but general lakes
!   (natural lakes, lagoons, regulating ponds, and lake groups sharing
!   one level) are handled by the same mechanism.
!   The surface is specified in one of three ways (per number n):
!     dam_in_cell (namelist) / fn_dam_in_cell (file)
!       : the capture band of a dam (a line crossing just upstream of
!         the dam body; specify with generous margin in wetted width so
!         that it catches all water from upstream)
!     fn_dam_map (lake-number raster; one file shared by all lakes)
!       : a surface resolved by the raster. <= 0 = none (negative
!         nodata ignored), value n = the surface of structure n.
!         The same number on detached bodies = a level-sharing group
!   Note (diagonal leakage): specify the capture set 4-neighbor
!   connected (diagonal-only corners can be slipped through by diagonal
!   links; the program does not check = user's responsibility)
!   HV (level-storage; levels are absolute elevations):
!     dam_hv               : the HV polyline from references (at least
!                            2 points = lowest level and surcharge;
!                            more allowed, linear interpolation). For dams
!     dam_hmin (+dam_hsur) : auto-built linear HV V(H)=A x (H-hmin),
!                            A = number of capture cells x cell area.
!                            No bathymetry needed = for lakes. hmin only
!                            places the drawdown floor; any value below
!                            the normal level gives identical results
!                            (about normal level - 5 m is fine).
!                            dam_hsur omitted = no upper bound (no
!                            spill; mandatory for modes 1,2 and the
!                            sqrt law)
!     neither              : accepted only for pass-through
!                            (f_dam_mode=2, dam_rate=1.0)
!   Operation mode f_dam_mode:
!     1: constant release dam_q0 (m3/s)
!     2: constant-rate cut dam_rate (release ratio 0-1; releases the
!        inflow of that step x r. rate=1.0 is pass-through = storage
!        untouched, inflow released as is)
!     3: natural regulation (a level-release rating). Specify one of the
!        following (in order of precedence):
!        (a) dam_hq_rule (polyline of water level m, release m3/s)
!        (b) the orifice dimensions dam_ori_width/height/zbase
!            (+ optional ce)
!        (c) dam_qmax (design maximum release = at surcharge; available
!            in dam yearbooks)
!            -> automatically builds the square root law
!            Q(H)=Qmax*sqrt((H-invert)/(Hsur-invert))
!            (if the invert elevation dam_zbase is omitted = the lowest
!            level. Needs a surcharge level)
!     none (and no release cells): a level-held lake (all inflow
!        vanishes; the level stays at dam_h_init = not lowered even by
!        evaporation. A "large water surface not solved")
!   Multiple release works: dam_lake(n) = m makes structure n draw from
!   the storage of lake m with its own rule and release cells (an
!   additional release work. The referenced lake must be a
!   lower-numbered lake with a main release. Draws are applied in
!   number order; the spill goes out through the main release. No
!   surface / dam_hv / dam_hmin / dam_h_init on the referencing side)
!   Groundwater-head forcing: dam_gw(n) = 1 fixes the groundwater head
!   on the surface cells to the lake level (Dirichlet. Requires
!   f_gwlateral=1 of fn_gwflow. Storage lakes trade the exchange with
!   V; level-held lakes only record it. Written to the Qgw column of
!   the CSV. The head saturates at the ground surface = the excess
!   pressure of the lake depth is not represented)
!   Emergency release (modes 1,2): from dam_tadashigaki (start level m;
!   if omitted = lowest + 0.9 x (surcharge - lowest)) up to the
!   surcharge, the release is ramped up toward the inflow. The excess
!   above the surcharge is forced spill (automatic).
!   Initial level dam_h_init (if omitted = the lowest level = empty.
!   For multipurpose dams the restricted level, for storage lakes the
!   normal level, explicitly). Drawdown floor = the storage at the
!   lowest level (dead storage). Keeping the normal level is the job of
!   the rating (the level of Q=0 = the outlet sill).
!   Evaporation (with &list_evap active): from the capture set storage
!   cell by cell by the cell area (same as ponds). Painting the surface
!   on the raster makes the impounded area = cells x cell area.
!   (The former dam_area was removed on 2026-08-20 = specifying it
!   stops with a read error.)
!   At every record time, (t, H, V, Qin, Qout, Qspill) is written to
!   result/dams/dam0001.csv (Qin is the direct accumulation of the
!   capture set absorption = exact; for a level-held lake the vanished
!   volume).
!   Constraints: neither surface nor release cells can be combined
!   with ponds (rscap>0) or channel-width cells. A multi-cell release
!   is divided equally by the number of cells. Absorption of direct
!   rainfall and injection on the capture set lags by one step.
!   Represent large lakes (Lake Biwa class) as storage lakes (mode 3),
!   not pass-through (the storage attenuation A dH/dt = Qin - Q(H) is
!   the leading control of the flood response)
!======================================================================
!&list_struct_dam
!  fn_dam_map = "lake_map.txt"           ! lake-number raster (used by lakes 2, 4)
!  ! Dam 1: a flood control dam with a constant 70% cut (release ratio
!  ! 0.3; the HV curve from references)
!  dam_in_cell(:,1,1) = 120, 44          ! capture band (a column crossing the
!                                        !   dam lake)
!  dam_in_cell(:,2,1) = 120, 45
!  dam_in_cell(:,3,1) = 120, 46
!  dam_out_cell(:,1,1) = 123, 45         ! release cell (the channel just
!                                        !   downstream of the dam body)
!  dam_hv(:,1,1) = 320.0, 0.0            ! lowest level and its storage
!  dam_hv(:,2,1) = 355.0, 1.5e7          ! surcharge level and total storage
!  f_dam_mode(1) = 2
!  dam_rate(1) = 0.3
!  dam_h_init(1) = 340.0                 ! initial level (default = lowest = empty)
!  ! Lake 2: a storage lake on the raster surface. Natural regulation
!  ! (H-Q rating) + auto linear HV
!  dam_hmin(2) = 81.0                    ! lower-bound level (insensitive if below
!                                        !   the normal level)
!  f_dam_mode(2) = 3
!  dam_hq_rule(:,1,2) = 84.0, 0.0        ! level of Q=0 = outlet sill ~ normal level
!  dam_hq_rule(:,2,2) = 86.0, 400.0      ! (level m, release m3/s)
!  dam_h_init(2) = 84.2                  ! initial level = the normal level
!  dam_out_cell(:,1,2) = 210, 95         ! the most upstream cell of the outlet river
!  ! Lake 3: pass-through (a connecting water surface with negligible
!  ! retention. No HV needed)
!  dam_in_cell(:,1,3) = 60, 22
!  dam_in_cell(:,2,3) = 60, 23
!  f_dam_mode(3) = 2
!  dam_rate(3) = 1.0
!  dam_out_cell(:,1,3) = 63, 22
!  ! Lake 4: a level-held lake (a large surface not solved; inflow
!  ! vanishes, level fixed). The lakeside groundwater head is also fixed
!  ! to the lake level (needs the lateral flow of fn_gwflow)
!  dam_h_init(4) = 12.0                  ! the fixed level (surface = raster number 4)
!  dam_gw(4) = 1                         ! fix the groundwater head to 12.0
!  ! Structure 5: an additional release work of lake 2 (dam_lake
!  ! reference = several releases with different operations. The
!  ! referenced lake must be a lower-numbered lake with a main release.
!  ! HV and levels are shared with lake 2)
!  dam_lake(5) = 2                       ! draws from the storage of lake 2
!  f_dam_mode(5) = 1
!  dam_q0(5) = 15.0                      ! constant canal intake 15 m3/s
!  dam_out_cell(:,1,5) = 150, 120        ! the inlet cell of the canal
!/
