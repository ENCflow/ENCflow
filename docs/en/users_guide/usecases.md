# Use cases (lookup by phenomenon)

> English mirror of docs/users_guide/usecases.md. The Japanese file is the master copy.

[Back to the User's Guide index](../users_guide.md)

A lookup table from "the phenomenon you want to compute" to the
combination of features and the key settings. See each chapter for
details and the [full parameter index](params_index.md) for exact
parameter meanings. **Phenomena not yet supported (planned)** are
listed explicitly at the end.

The notes column contains only what a user should know for that use
case - the nature of the approximation, how to supply the data, and
accuracy caveats (blank = nothing special).

## Floods and inundation

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| River (fluvial) flood inundation | [Geographic information](geoinfo.md) + [Boundary conditions](boundary.md) (+ [Channels](channel.md), [Structures](structure.md)) | Terrain + inflow time series (bc_qin / inflow) | Preprocess real terrain with depression removal (utils/rmdepress_river). See the tutorial for discharge oscillations on flat channel reaches |
| Rain-on-grid inundation (pluvial) | [Rainfall and weather](forcing.md) | Put fn_precip (uniform/distributed) directly on the terrain | Represent losses physically with infiltration (Green-Ampt in [Groundwater](gwflow.md)) rather than runoff coefficients |
| Dam / farm-pond breach flood | Levees + breach in [Channels](channel.md) (+ initial condition) | Represent the reservoir as channel cells (rw) and the dam body as a levee (bank); give the breach as a crest time series (&list_channel_breach) | The breach widening process is an input (time series); erosion-driven self-widening is not predicted |
| River levee breach flooding | Breach in [Channels](channel.md) (virtual wall) or [Prescribed bed motion](bedmotion.md) (resolved levee) | For a virtual-wall levee, the crest time series of &list_channel_breach. For a levee present as elevation in a high-resolution DEM (a resolved levee), lower the bed of the breach cells along a time history (make_breach.py writes the table and the windowed displacement files). Example: [examples/levee_breach](../../../examples/levee_breach/en/README.md) | The progression of the breach width and depth is an input (erosion-driven self-widening is not predicted). The resolved-levee breach is a prescribed change of shape and does not change the soil depth |
| Open levees (kasumi-tei), overflow levees, secondary levees, ring levees | Levees in [Channels](channel.md) | fn_bank / bank0 + openings f_bank_opening | Overflow widths, crest heights, and opening locations are given as data |
| Detention basins / retarding ponds | Terrain + [Channels](channel.md) + [Structures](structure.md) | Overflow levee (bank) + intake / drainage gates (culvert/gate) | |
| Urban flooding by wave overtopping | Source in [Boundary conditions](boundary.md) | Feed the overtopping discharge time series into cells behind the seawall with &list_bound_source | Waves and the overtopping rate themselves are not computed (supply externally estimated values) |

## Coasts and estuaries

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storm-surge / tsunami run-up | [Tide and sea level](tide.md) | Sea mask + tide/water-level time series | Offshore generation and propagation are received as water levels from outside (to compute the generation itself, see the next row) |
| Tsunami generation by an earthquake (fault): generation, propagation, run-up, and coastal uplift/subsidence | [Prescribed bed motion](bedmotion.md) + a water body (initial level f_htype=2, the "solved sea" without a sea mask) + [boundary conditions](boundary.md) (long-wave radiation at the edges) | Convert the fault parameters (position, strike, dip, rake, length, width, slip, rupture time, rise time) into a snapshot table of ground displacement with utils/fault2disp and give it to fn_bedmotion. Longitude/latitude, projected coordinates or a georeferenced grid all work. Examples: [examples/tsunami_fault](../../../examples/tsunami_fault/en/README.md) (idealised fault), [examples/tsunami_coast](../../../examples/tsunami_coast/en/README.md) (coastal subsidence/uplift and the offshore tsunami with their time lag) | Land and sea cells move with the same displacement field, so the flooding of subsided lowland, the waves spreading from uplifted areas and their superposition in bays are all in one run. The difference from an instantaneous displacement (the initial-surface approach) is the near-field timing (tens of seconds to minutes of rupture propagation). The hydrostatic long-wave approximation suffices when the source width ≫ depth; for narrow deformation (splay faults, submarine slides) use the non-hydrostatic f_nh_bottom in the near field |
| Timber influx from coastal log yards by storm surge (Isewan-typhoon type) | [Driftwood](driftwood.md) + [Tide and sea level](tide.md) | Log yard and town as normal cells + initial water level (sea mask offshore only). Give the timber stock in the yard cells and use hydraulic recruitment as "mooring loss by the surge" (dw_hrec above the normal yard depth). Worked example: [examples/timberyard](../../../examples/timberyard/en/README.md) (README with figures and sensitivity cases) | Up to arrival/deposition maps (Wd9999). Individual impact forces are out of scope, but the inputs of the design impact formula (velocity × representative diameter) are available from the outputs (postprocessing) |
| Destruction of wooden houses by tsunami or storm surge and the transport/deposition of debris (including the added destructive force of driftwood) | [Building destruction and debris](bldgdebris.md) (+ [Driftwood](driftwood.md) + [Tide and sea level](tide.md)) | Give the building stock fn_bdstock (wooden fraction included) and the destructible share fn_bdfrac; destruction by inundation depth (fragility-function type) or by load (driftwood and debris included). The void-ratio feedback f_bdgv returns destruction to the flow. Worked example: [examples/tsunami_town](../../../examples/tsunami_town/en/README.md) | Up to damage-ratio maps (Bs), debris arrival/deposit maps (Bd9999) and the fluid force including floating matter (Fd9999). Individual houses, debris pieces and impact forces are out of scope (impact loads are postprocessing) |
| River tsunami (tsunami intrusion up a river) | [Tide and sea level](tide.md) + [Channels](channel.md) | Tsunami waveform on the sea cells at the river mouth | One water level per cell inside the channel (no cross-sectional variation). For soliton fission and undular bores during the intrusion see the next row |
| Dispersive tsunamis and waves (soliton fission of river-intruding tsunamis, undular bores, solitary waves, the near field of short sources and landslides) | The non-hydrostatic correction f_nonhydrostatic in [Shallow water flow](swflow.md) (+ f_nh_slope bottom-slope terms, f_nh_bottom moving-bottom acceleration, f_nh_breaking breaking switch, nh_break_visc breaking eddy viscosity) | A one-layer non-hydrostatic projection (research option; the default stays hydrostatic). Grid spacing of 1/4 to 1/8 of the depth and Courant ≲ 0.1 are the guide. The active set f_nh_adaptive=1 solves only where needed. Verification: [test/nhwave_nh](../../../test/nhwave_nh/en/README.md) (standing waves), [nhsolitary](../../../test/nhsolitary/en/README.md) (solitary wave), [nhshelf](../../../test/nhshelf/en/README.md) and [nhbore](../../../test/nhbore/en/README.md) (soliton fission), [nhbreak](../../../test/nhbreak/en/README.md) (breaking run-up), [nhbottom](../../../test/nhbottom/en/README.md) (moving bottom) | The dispersion relation is the one-layer one (valid for kH ≲ 2). Breaking run-up with the breaking switch + eddy viscosity + friction is within +8% of the empirical law. The iterative solver costs 2 to 10 times the hydrostatic run |
| Tsunami resuspension of bay-bottom mud / sand and deposition in the run-up zone (tsunami deposits) | f_suspend in [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (+ f_fluvial for sand) | Represent the bay not as a sea mask but as ordinary cells + initial water level f_htype=2, and let the tsunami enter through the long-wave radiation edge boundary; the erodible layer is the soil depth sd (details in the suspended-sediment section of geomorph.md) | Landform change does not act on sea-mask cells, so how the water body is represented is the key point. Sand needs no calibration (Itakura-Kishi, f_esform=2); mud is calibrated via susp_wf / susp_esa / susp_tausc (Partheniades-type reinterpretation). Single grain size - scope is deposit thickness and extent (inland fining is not represented) |
| Compound flooding (surge x river flood x heavy rain) | Superposition of tide + boundaries + rainfall | Just enable the features together | The ordering and coupling between processes is automatic (single time evolution) |
| Seawater intrusion into / retreat from aquifers | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) + [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | fn_salt (f_salt_gw=1) + f_gwlateral=1 + sea mask | Sharp-interface approximation: no brackish water (mixing) is produced. Equilibrium follows Ghyben-Herzberg |
| Pumping-induced seawater intrusion / well salinization | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) + pumping sink in [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | The above + f_gwpump=1 (coastal wells) | Pumping takes fresh water first (shallow well screen approximation); whatever the fresh thickness cannot supply appears as salt contamination of the well, and the induced wedge movement / upconing emerge automatically from the lateral flow and the salt-layer dynamics |
| Freshwater lenses (islands, sand bars) | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) + rainfall/infiltration | Recharge (rain + Green-Ampt) + sea mask | Lens thickness is set by the recharge-to-K_sh ratio (spin-up recommended) |
| Estuarine salt wedge | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) (f_salt_surf=1) | River inflow (fresh) + tide (salt) | Targets the quasi-steady wedge position and its response (no internal-wave inertia). The advection share salt_alpha is the practical calibration point |
| Tidal response of coastal aquifers | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) + [Tide and sea level](tide.md) | The tide series directly becomes the subsurface sea-side boundary head | The sea-subsurface connection exists only through the fresh/salt module |
| Where run-up seawater ends up (areal salt distribution) | [Fresh and salt water layers](salt.md) + [Tide and sea level](tide.md) | Run-up seawater is tracked automatically as salt water (Hss output) | Currently up to the surface extent; the path where it infiltrates and contaminates soil/aquifer (complete salt-damage analysis) is planned (below) |
| Polder / lowland drainage by pumping stations | Levees in [Channels](channel.md) + [Structures](structure.md) + [Tide and sea level](tide.md) | Enclose with levees and pump to the outer water (place the outlet on a channel cell; to discharge to the sea, omit the outlet = removal from the domain; no structure can be placed on a cell of the sea mask) | Example of a pumping station drawing directly from the conduits and of a sea outfall: [test/coastal_drain](../../../test/coastal_drain/en/README.md) |
| Distribution of coastal wave height and direction (shoaling, refraction, breaking, shadows of breakwaters and islands) | [Coastal wave field](wavefield.md) (fn_wavefield) | Represent the offshore not as a sea mask but as a "solved sea" of ordinary cells + an initial water level f_htype=2. Make the offshore side incident (wf_bc_side = 1) and the left and right sides crossing the coast lateral Neumann (2). Offshore waves by wf_hm0, wf_trep, wf_dir (, wf_spread) or the time series wf_val. The quasi-steady mode (default) updates every dt_wave. Verification: [test/wfshoal](../../../test/wfshoal/en/README.md) (shoaling, refraction, breaking), [test/wfshadow](../../../test/wfshadow/en/README.md) (breakwater shadow) | Phase-averaged, one representative period, directional bins (the same level as the XBeach stationary mode and SnapWave). No diffraction, reflection or wind growth (the smearing of shadow edges is numerical diffusion). The action on the bed is the row below (f_wfsed; simple littoral drift without solving the waves is fn_wave) |
| Wave setup, longshore currents and undertow | [Coastal wave field](wavefield.md#action-on-the-flow-wf_flow) (`wf_flow = 2`) + the lateral Neumann edge boundary of the flow (`f_bc_*` = 3 in [boundary conditions](boundary.md)) | The configuration of the row above plus `wf_flow = 2` (force + GLM Stokes transport + wave–current friction). The left and right sides crossing the coast are lateral Neumann for both waves and flow. The output velocities u and v are Lagrangian means (the flow at the bed is u − Wsx). Verification: [test/wfcurrent](../../../test/wfcurrent/en/README.md) (setup, longshore current, roller) | Radiation stress (waves + roller), GLM and wave–current friction are the same family as the XBeach stationary mode. No wave-induced horizontal mixing by default (the Battjes 1975 type can be added with `wf_break_visc`). Waves with zero directional spreading refract too slowly, producing a false alongshore force offshore of the surf zone (give a directional spreading) |
| Waves on river mouths, tidal currents and rip currents (blocking of propagation up a river, steepening, refraction by currents) | [Coastal wave field](wavefield.md#change-of-waves-by-currents-f_wf_current) (`f_wf_current = 1`) | The configuration of the row above plus `f_wf_current = 1`; also `wf_flow = 2` for two-way coupling with the wave-driven flow. Blocked wave action appears as blocked in the ledger `wavefield.csv`. Verification: [test/wfdoppler](../../../test/wfdoppler/en/README.md) (opposing and following currents, blocking, river-mouth jet) | With one representative period, blocking happens to all the energy at once. No reflection at the blocking point. The memory of the wave field becomes about 3.6 times as large. Near blocking the quasi-steady iteration converges slowly (raise `wf_itmax`) |
| Longshore transport, beach erosion and recovery (process-based sediment transport by the wave field) | [Coastal wave field](wavefield.md#action-on-sediment-f_wfsed) (`f_wfsed = 1` of fn_geomorph) | The configuration of the row above (`wf_flow = 2`) plus the bed load and suspended load of fn_geomorph with `f_wfsed = 1`. The left and right sides crossing the coast lateral Neumann (zero gradient for sediment too). The longshore transport rate is the cross-shore integral of Qby and Qsy (`f_wf_outdiag = 1`). Verification: [test/wfsed](../../../test/wfsed/en/README.md) (stirring, longshore transport against CERC, erosion and recovery, conservation of sediment) | Stirring by the orbital velocity only by default (breaking turbulence with `f_wfsed_turb`), no horizontal diffusion of suspended load. Piling up above the shoreline (land building) needs the swash-zone transport `f_wfswash = 1` (empirical formulas; give the equilibrium slope `wfswash_tanbe`; verification: [test/wfswash](../../../test/wfswash/en/README.md)). The balance between erosion and recovery is sensitive to the coefficients `wfsed_facsk` and `wfsed_facas` (calibration quantities). Exclusive with the longshore transport of fn_wave |
| Arcuate / cuspate deltas, shoreline smoothing, spits (littoral drift by waves) | fn_wave of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (+ bed load f_fluvial) | Give the offshore significant wave height, period and direction (fixed values or a time series) with fn_wave. f_fluvial=1 is required. Represent the sea not as a sea mask but as ordinary cells + an initial water level | The littoral drift of the CERC formula from offshore waves is applied to the bed as bed load in the surf zone. No diffraction (a simple exposure treatment); cross-shore transport, set-up and rip currents are out of scope. Any water deeper than the surf zone becomes a wave source, so either make the water bodies that are not of interest a sea mask or shadow them by the wave direction |

## Urban

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pluvial flooding including sewer drainage and surcharge (dual drainage) | Conduit continuum layer in [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | f_gwconduit=1 + capacity, conveyance, inlet density | Reproduces the areal drainage capacity and the spatial pattern of surcharge (does not identify *which* manhole erupts). Networks dominated by operated structures (pumps, CSOs) or by a single trunk main are out of scope |
| Storage effect of underground detention / storm trunks | Conduit continuum layer | Give large cap and conveyance along the relevant cell strings | Tracking a single trunk main is a weak point (continuum approximation) |
| Rainwater storage/infiltration facilities, green infrastructure, permeable pavement | [Structures](structure.md) and ponds (rscap) + infiltration maps ([Groundwater](gwflow.md)) | Represent storage with structures/ponds, and infiltration with map input of the Green-Ampt parameters (fn_gw_ksv / fn_gw_psif) | Give the K_sv map by land use, from 0 (fully paved = impervious) up to large values (infiltration facilities). The internal structure of a facility is not represented (expressed as per-cell infiltration capacity and storage) |
| Sanitary risk of flood water from sewer surcharge (coliforms etc.) | Conduit continuum layer + the conduit coupling of [Water quality](wq.md) | wq_gwc_conc (fixed concentration of surcharge / outfall water) + first-order decay (T90 → wq_k20) | Fixed supply-side concentration approximation (the budget of bacteria inside the pipes is not solved). Unit reading, representative values and how to read the output: see "Sanitary risk of sewer surcharge" in the wq chapter |

|---|---|---|---|

## Sediment, slopes, and volcanoes

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rainfall-induced slope failure -> debris flow -> inundation | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) + [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | f_slide + infiltration (Green-Ampt) + lateral flow | The stability judgment is driven by the groundwater level (pore pressure) - enable infiltration to make it truly "rainfall-induced" |
| Sector collapse -> channel blockage (landslide dam) -> outburst flood | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) | f_release / f_slide + debris flow + deposition (path entrainment via f_dbed=4) | The deposited terrain dams the flow automatically (two-way coupling of terrain and flow) |
| Effect of sabo facilities (sediment-retarding basins, check dams) | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (+ [Structures](structure.md), [Channels](channel.md)) | Two-case comparison with / without the facility. A retarding basin is carved into the terrain; a closed check dam is non-erodible terrain (add to z with sd=0); an open (slit) dam is the dam body + a culvert (passes water only = upper-bound estimate of trapping). See "Representing sabo facilities" in geomorph.md | Sedimentation -> filling -> crest overflow evolves automatically. Single grain size, so size-selective trapping is not represented (the map-scale trapped wood volume can be assessed by adding [Driftwood](driftwood.md)). Results are bit-reproducible regardless of rank count, so the difference between the two cases is exactly the facility's effect, separated from numerical noise |
| Landslide / sector-collapse-generated tsunamis (surges in reservoirs, landslide-dammed lakes and bays), submarine-landslide tsunamis | The moving bed layer f_bedslide of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) + a water body (initial level f_htype=2, the "solved sea" without a sea mask + long-wave radiation at the edges) | Give the failing mass (planar slip surface) with fn_bsinit (no extra settings - the run-out on land and under water, the wave generation by the rise and fall of the bed, propagation and run-up are automatic). Run a water-rich debris flow as a mixture with fn_dbinit and hand it over to the bed layer with bs_hplunge. Examples: [examples/landslide_tsunami](../../../examples/landslide_tsunami/en/README.md) (4 patterns + non-hydrostatic versions) | The bed layer runs even under water where the surface is flat (driving head including buoyancy), and the rise and fall of the bed displaces the water surface while the depth is preserved (the moving-seabed approach). Being inertia-free (terminal velocity from the moment of release), near-field waveforms and peak heights are rough estimates (reservoirs, narrow bays and shallow coasts are the right scale). For scales that need dispersion (slide length within a few times the depth) use the non-hydrostatic correction (param_*_nh): the far-field leading wave is halved by dispersion, and the acceleration term f_nh_bottom acts just above the deposit but leaves a self-excited oscillation with the bed layer (limitations in the example README). A water-rich debris flow can also be made to run under water by running it with the debris-flow model (fn_dbinit) and handing it over to the bed layer in the standing water (bs_hplunge) |
| Floods with driftwood; mixed debris-flow-and-driftwood surges | [Driftwood](driftwood.md) (+ [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md)) | Standing stock (dw_stock0 / fn_dwstock) + recruitment (hydraulic washout dw_wrec, erosion entrainment dw_droot) + stopping (dw_wstop). For the debris-flow mixture just enable f_debris together | A raster-field estimate of wood volume (Wd9999 = maximum arrival = the driftwood hazard map). Individual-log tracking, geometric jamming at bridges/slit dams and impact forces are out of scope (approximate blockage effects by two-case comparisons with narrowed openings) |
| Reservoir sedimentation and sediment flushing | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) + [Structures](structure.md) | Suspended load (f_suspend) + dam operation | Flushing-gate operation is given as a time series |
| Riverbed evolution / river-mouth bar flushing | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (+ [Tide and sea level](tide.md)) | Bedload (f_fluvial) + suspended load (f_suspend) + a flood hydrograph. Represent the nearshore not as a sea mask but as ordinary cells + initial water level f_htype=2, and give the bar's erodible volume as the soil depth sd (see "The sea mask and the fate of sediment" in geomorph.md) | The positive feedback of overtopping -> incision -> flow concentration is automatic through the two-way coupling of terrain and flow. Bank collapse can be represented with f_repose (angle of repose) and f_berode (bank erosion); see the meandering-channel row below. If the crest is uniform, give a low point where the breach is expected. In rapid flushing fluv_dzmax can become the rate limiter. Post-flush bar recovery can be represented by the littoral drift of fn_wave (cross-shore transport is out of scope) |
| Formation of a river-mouth delta (mouth bar -> bifurcation -> lobe growth -> channel switching; fan versus bird-foot by the sand/mud ratio; stabilisation of distributaries by discharge variation and vegetation fixation) | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) bed load + suspended load (+ bed-fines ledger) + angle-of-repose avalanching (+ fixation, bank erosion) + [Boundary conditions](boundary.md) (long-wave radiation on an edge, sediment feed of the section inflow) | Represent the sea as ordinary cells + an initial water level f_htype=2, not as a sea mask, with the far edge as long-wave radiation (f_bc=2; the reference level is derived from e0). Sand by f_fluvial + capacity feed fluv_bcfeed=1, mud by the inflow concentration inflow_cs of f_suspend (give the settling velocity susp_wf directly) + f_bedfines. fluv_slope=1 and f_repose=1 are essential, f_secflow is not. morfac 100-200. For the persistence of distributaries combine alternating floods and low flows with f_fixation=1 (fix_tdec for the decay while submerged, fix_ctau_bank to separate the effect on banks). Procedure in geomorph.md "River-mouth deltas" | Sea cells accept no sediment, so the representation of the water body is the key point. The symmetry breaking is not random but the instability of the bifurcation (amplifying round-off): the shape differs between realisations, the integrated quantities do not; argue with the statistics of several realisations. The effect of the sand/mud ratio has the same sign as in DeltaRCM, Delft3D and experiments. Resolve the distributaries with at least width/5 cells. Waves only as far as the littoral drift of fn_wave (arcuate and cuspate deltas). Tidal currents, plume buoyancy and consolidation are out of scope. Record of the trials: docs/delta_plan.md (Japanese) |
| Development of meandering channels (outer-bank retreat with inner-bank bar growth, bars turning into land, neck cutoff, bars merging into the floodplain) | The bedload part of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (direction correction, bank erosion, angle-of-repose avalanching, fixation) + [suspended sediment](geomorph.md) (+ the ledger of fines) | f_fluvial=1 + fluv_slope=1 (bed-slope term) + f_secflow=1 (equilibrium secondary flow) + f_repose=1 (angle-of-repose avalanching) + f_berode=1 (cohesive banks). Alternating floods and low flows with f_fixation=1 turn inner-bank bars into land; capacity feed fluv_bcfeed=1 removes the degradation wave of a no-feed inlet. To raise bars to floodplain level, add suspended load f_suspend=1 with equilibrium-concentration feed (inflow_cs_eq) and f_bedfines=1 (ledger of fines in the bed). Examples: [examples/meander](../../../examples/meander/en/README.md) (9 configurations) and [examples/meander_cutoff](../../../examples/meander_cutoff/en/README.md) (neck cutoff) | Banks are the step between neighbouring cells, not a vector line (raster purity). Outer-bank retreat is the chain pool deepening -> bank-face collapse -> removal by the toe flow, and cutoffs emerge from wetting-drying and landform change. N*, μs, μk, the fixation time and c_τ have no defaults pending literature verification and must be given explicitly (candidate values in geomorph.md). In an incised channel without a channel mask the default f_opening_dynamic=2 carries the toe flow. Non-equilibrium secondary flow and the evaluation of avulsion are planned (developer.md §84) |
| Lahars, post-ashfall rain-triggered mudflows and snowmelt-type mudflows | The "Lahars" paragraph of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) + snow ([Rainfall and weather](forcing.md)) | A mixture whose concentration changes (f_dbed=1/2 + f_dbres=4 or 1, f_dbwet=1). Initiated by erosion of the ash layer (sd), a sediment-laden segment inflow or a breach chain; give meltwater as an inflow. Example: [examples/ashfall_lahar](../../../examples/ashfall_lahar/en/README.md) | Dilute systems (pyroclastic surges, ash transport) and thermal interaction are out of scope |
| Lava-flow inundation and lava-field formation -> subsequent rainfall runoff | [Lava flows](lavaflow.md) | Vent cells + effusion time series + η, τ_y (+ solidification lv_wsol/lv_vsol) | Solidified lava becomes the bed z, and rain, floods, and sediment flow over the new topography in the same run. Isothermal (cooling and temperature-dependent viscosity are future extensions). Thermal lava-water interaction is out of scope |
| Valley formation on badlands and pyroclastic cones (highly permeable slopes with no surface runoff) | [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) | f_splash (dry-slope erosion) + infiltration (Green-Ampt); the valley spacing is selected by spl_lc. Examples: [examples/badland](../../../examples/badland/en/README.md) (deep valleys with sharp ridges), [examples/badland_round](../../../examples/badland_round/en/README.md) (swelling-clay type with rounded crests where lower hills become domes; adds f_creep) | Even where all rainfall infiltrates and no overland flow occurs, valleys grow from cliff margins through the hollow-amplification feedback of rainsplash + subgrid rills. Cohesion (combine with f_slide) decides whether valleys form at all. Rounded crests follow from the length scale L = D/(P kt S^(h−1)) set by the ratio of diffusion (creep) to advective incision: hills smaller than L round off first |

## Snow and ice

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snowmelt floods, rain-on-snow | Snow in [Rainfall and weather](forcing.md) | fn_snow + air temperature (lapse rate) | Degree-day method (energy balance not implemented) |
| Infiltration suppression by frozen ground (meltwater running over frozen soil) | Frozen ground (f_gwfrost) in [Groundwater](gwflow.md) + temperature (+ snow) | f_gwfrost=1 (fro_fifull defaults to 20 degC.day; + fro_swe0 for snow insulation, fro_fi0 to start "already frozen") | The simplest model - a degree-day freezing index reduces the infiltration capacity (soil temperature and frost depth are not solved). fro_fifull is the practical calibration point |
| Glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF) | [Glaciers](glacier.md) + terrain + breach in [Channels](channel.md) | Represent the glacial lake as impounded terrain; give the breach as a crest time series | The breach trigger and widening are inputs |
| Glacier retreat / cirque formation (long term) | [Glaciers](glacier.md) + long runs | Repeated representative years x morfac x restart chains | |
| Snow avalanche (dense flow) runout and deposition, with path entrainment | Equivalent fluid in [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) (snow-avalanche paragraph) | f_dbed=4 (velocity-proportional entrainment) + f_dbres=4 (Voellmy) + fn_dbinit (release area + fracture depth) + sd (path snow depth) | Release is given as a scenario (the same practice as dedicated avalanche models). Powder-snow avalanches are out of scope. See the "Snow avalanches (dense flow)" paragraph in the geomorph chapter for the typical setup and limits. Example: [test/avalanche](../../../test/avalanche/en/README.md) |

## Water quality

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pollutant load runoff / first flush | [Water quality](wq.md) | Buildup-washoff + advection | |
| Coliform, nutrient, pesticide runoff (first-order approximation) | [Water quality](wq.md) | Match decay coefficients and settling velocities to the substance | Reactions are first-order decay, settling and Kd equilibrium partitioning only (no ecosystem / reaction networks). Concentrations are fully mixed (no vertical profile) |
| Basin-scale transport of sorbing substances such as heavy metals | [Water quality](wq.md) + [Sediment](geomorph.md) | wq_kd (equilibrium two-phase partitioning) + f_suspend + f_wq_settle=1 | Dissolved/particulate phases partitioned continuously by Kd. One run per metal. Take Kd from the literature or observed particulate/dissolved ratios |
| Dry-weather (baseflow) river quality | [Water quality](wq.md) + [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | f_gwlateral=1 + f_wq_infil=1 (sorption retardation via wq_rg) | Closes the mass pathway infiltration → groundwater flow → seepage |
| Loads passing through reservoir / pond chains | [Water quality](wq.md) + [Structures](structure.md) | Automatic with dams/lakes and rscap (complete mixing M/V) | Attenuation and delay of tank cascades. In-reservoir settling is not implemented (release concentration on the safe side) |

## Long-term landforms

| Phenomenon | Features | Key settings | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landform evolution / erosion rates (hydraulics-driven LEM) | Long-term features of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) | Weathering, uplift + cyclic forcing + morfac | |
| Karst landforms (doline fields, internal drainage) | Dissolution f_dissol of [Sediment and landform change](geomorph.md) + the conduit continuum layer of [Groundwater](gwflow.md) | Dissolution (proportional to recharge) + sink-inflow term dissol_sink + inlet density + cyclic forcing + morfac. Lateral dissolution dissol_lat = −1 and rock-wall collapse f_rockfall for funnel shapes. Example: [examples/karst](../../../examples/karst/en/README.md) | Cave geometry is out of scope. The upper bound of morfac is set by the positive feedback within one storm (see the example) |
| Tower karst (towers left on a plain) | Dissolution f_dissol (dissol_lat) + rock-wall collapse f_rockfall + a conductivity map | Bare-rock towers (low conductivity) and a soil-covered plain (high conductivity). The water infiltrating on the plain and at the feet dissolves; the walls retreat by collapse. No sinks, so infiltration caps the intake and morfac 1e4 is usable. Example: [examples/tower_karst](../../../examples/tower_karst/en/README.md) | The basal notch comes from lateral dissolution, the vertical walls from the stable rock angle of the collapse |

## Not yet supported (planned)

The following are recognized user needs, planned as extensions that fit
the existing framework (order and timing undecided).

| Phenomenon | Current state | Planned form |
|---|---|---|
| Wind-driven flow / wind setup (bays, lakes, wide floodplains) | No wind stress term | Wind stress in the momentum equations + wind input (extension of the weather forcing) |
| Complete salt-damage analysis (contamination of soil/aquifer by infiltrated seawater) | Possible up to the areal extent of run-up seawater (above) | Fresh/salt partitioning of infiltration and evapotranspiration |
| Paddy field dams (runoff suppression by outlet restriction) | Can be approximated by storage + orifices, but no standard recipe | Under consideration |
| Powder-snow avalanches / automatic chaining from snowpack (SWE) runs | Dense-flow runout with path entrainment is available (see Snow and ice above); powder avalanches are outside the shallow-water approximation | Powder avalanches remain out of scope. Automatic SWE-to-entrainable-layer (sd) conversion is under consideration (currently done in preprocessing) |
